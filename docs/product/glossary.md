# Glossary — MediHistory AI

**Document version:** 1.0  
**Status:** Draft

This glossary defines the domain terms used consistently across all MediHistory AI artifacts: requirements, architecture, design, code, and tests. When a term has a specific meaning in this project that differs from general usage, this document takes precedence.

---

## Active Ingredient

The pharmacologically active chemical substance in a medicine that produces the therapeutic effect. Used as the basis for duplicate detection rather than brand names. Example: "metformin" is the active ingredient in the brand "Glucophage."

Two medicines share the same active ingredient when their normalised active ingredient identity matches, regardless of brand, manufacturer, or trade name. The presence of the same active ingredient in two prescriptions from different doctors creates a potential duplicate alert but is not itself a clinical finding.

---

## Brand Name

The proprietary trade name assigned to a medicine by its manufacturer. A brand name may correspond to a single active ingredient (e.g. "Paracetamol 500 mg" = "Calpol") or a combination of active ingredients (e.g. "Co-amoxiclav" = amoxicillin + clavulanic acid). Brand names are not used as the sole basis for duplicate detection because the same active ingredient may appear under different brands, and different brands may share a name in different markets.

---

## Combination Medicine

A medicine that contains two or more active ingredients in a single dosage form. Example: a tablet containing both paracetamol and ibuprofen. Duplicate detection for combination medicines compares each constituent ingredient separately. A combination medicine with one matching ingredient generates a reduced-confidence alert, not a definitive duplicate.

---

## Confidence Score

A numeric value between 0.0 (no confidence) and 1.0 (maximum confidence) assigned to each field extracted by the OCR or AI pipeline. A confidence score represents the system's estimated probability that the extracted value matches what is written in the source document. It does not represent clinical accuracy. Fields below a project-defined threshold are highlighted for patient review.

---

## Consolidated Medicine List

The combined, deduplicated view of all medicines across all prescriptions stored in the app. Consolidation groups occurrences of the same active ingredient (regardless of brand) and presents them as a single medicine entry with multiple prescription occurrences. The consolidated list does not remove any prescription records; it is a display-layer aggregation only.

---

## Doctor-Ready Summary

A structured document generated by MediHistory AI that presents the patient's relevant medical history in a format suitable for handover to a new doctor. Includes documented conditions (from prescription context), medicines, prescription dates, and doctor information. Every statement is linked to its source document. The summary explicitly distinguishes documented facts, patient-reported information, uncertain extractions, and AI-generated text. It is a patient-compiled record, not a clinical report.

---

## Duplicate Alert (Potential)

A system-generated notification indicating that two or more prescriptions contain the same active ingredient. Always labelled as **potential** because the clinical significance of the overlap depends on factors not available to the app (e.g. dose, indication, patient physiology). A duplicate alert is not a medical finding and does not imply that the patient is at risk. It is a prompt for the patient to discuss the overlap with their doctor or pharmacist.

---

## Extraction

The process of identifying and structuring individual data fields (medicine name, active ingredient, strength, dosage, etc.) from the raw text output of OCR applied to a prescription image or PDF. Extraction is performed by an AI pipeline operating on untrusted input. Every extracted field stores its confidence score and review state. Extraction never infers or invents fields that are not present in the source document.

---

## Generic Name

The internationally standardised non-proprietary name for an active ingredient, as defined by the WHO International Nonproprietary Names (INN) programme or national equivalents. When a generic name is identifiable, it is preferred over brand name for normalisation and duplicate matching. Example: "metformin hydrochloride" (generic) vs. "Glucophage" (brand).

---

## Medical History

The patient's longitudinal record of documented conditions, medicines, prescriptions, allergies, and relevant clinical events as stored in MediHistory AI. In the context of this app, medical history refers specifically to the structured data compiled from the patient's own uploaded prescriptions and documents. It does not include data from external healthcare systems, clinical observations, or diagnoses inferred by the app.

---

## Normalization

The process of mapping a medicine name or active ingredient as extracted from a prescription (which may contain OCR errors, abbreviations, local trade names, or spelling variants) to a canonical active ingredient identity. Normalization is performed by the AI normalization service using a provider-independent interface. Normalized identity is used for duplicate matching. Normalization results are stored with confidence scores and are subject to review.

---

## OCR (Optical Character Recognition)

The automated process of converting a prescription image or scanned PDF into machine-readable text. OCR output is treated as untrusted input that may contain errors, omissions, ambiguities, or hallucinations introduced by image quality, handwriting, or service limitations. OCR output is never presented to patients or clinicians as verified information without confidence scoring and review.

---

## Patient

The person whose medical records are stored in the app. In the MVP, the patient is also the app user (the account holder). The patient has full ownership of their data and the right to review, edit, export, and delete it. The app treats the patient as a non-clinical end user who is not expected to have medical knowledge.

---

## Prescription

A record consisting of: the original document (image or PDF), metadata (doctor, date, specialty), and one or more PrescriptionMedicine entries extracted from the document. A prescription is immutable at the document level — the original file is never modified. Extracted data and patient corrections are stored separately and linked to the prescription.

---

## Review State

The processing state of an individual extracted field or PrescriptionMedicine record:

| State | Meaning |
|-------|---------|
| PENDING_REVIEW | Extracted by OCR/AI; not yet reviewed by the patient |
| REVIEWED | Patient has confirmed or corrected the extracted value |
| REJECTED | Patient has rejected the extracted value as incorrect; the field is excluded from downstream processing |

Review state transitions are: PENDING_REVIEW → REVIEWED, PENDING_REVIEW → REJECTED. A REVIEWED item can be moved back to PENDING_REVIEW if the patient requests re-extraction. A REJECTED item can be corrected and moved to REVIEWED by manual entry.

---

## Source Document

The original prescription image or PDF file as uploaded or captured by the patient. Source documents are stored in app-private storage and are never modified, deleted, or replaced by OCR or AI processing. Every extracted fact, medicine entry, and generated summary links back to its source document by document ID.

---

## Strength

The quantity of active ingredient per unit dose of a medicine, expressed in standard pharmaceutical notation (e.g. "500 mg", "5 mg/mL", "0.1%"). Strength is an extracted field, not an inferred field. When strength is not legible in the source document, the field is left empty. Strength is used alongside active ingredient in duplicate detection to provide context; different strengths of the same active ingredient still generate a potential duplicate alert.

---

## Synthetic Data

Fictitious patient names, medicine names, prescription dates, and clinical details used exclusively in test fixtures, UI demos, and development environments. Synthetic data must not contain any real patient identifiers, real prescription text, or real medicine dosages that could be misconstrued as actual medical advice. All test fixtures in the repository use synthetic data.

---

## Workaround

A temporary measure, limitation, or manual step required because an automated feature is not yet implemented or is unavailable. Workarounds are documented in the relevant acceptance criteria as known limitations. A workaround is not an acceptable long-term substitute for the specified feature.
